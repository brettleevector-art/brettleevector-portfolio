## 1. The north star

Create a **dark, premium, minimal, creative, technology-oriented portfolio** for Brettlee Sojan. The site should communicate work across **AI, data, machine learning, audio technology, research experimentation, and intelligent automation** without looking like a generic developer template.

The portfolio should feel like the work itself:

- **Scientific rigor** in the ML projects.
- **Systems thinking** in the automation workflows.
- **Creative taste** in Genesis and audio technology.

**That combination is the differentiator.** Make this idea clear in the website's structure, writing, and visual language—not as a loud marketing slogan.

The site should feel considered, technically literate, calm, editorial, and a little experimental. Prioritize strong typography, precise spacing, subtle texture, useful data visualization, clear writing, and restraint.

### Primary goals

1. Explain who Brettlee is and what kinds of problems she explores.
2. Make completed projects easy to inspect, especially WILDLIGHT and the International Space Station Tracker.
3. Make current experiments visible without presenting unfinished work as completed products.
4. Show the overlap between quantitative thinking, engineering, creative audio work, and automation systems.
5. Provide direct paths to GitHub, LinkedIn, email, and WhatsApp/contact.
6. Be credible to technical recruiters, AI/data teams, research-oriented engineers, creative technology studios, and early-stage product teams.

### What the site must not be

- No generic bootcamp/student portfolio template.
- No neon purple/blue gradient background.
- No loud multi-colour gradients, rainbow accents, glowing blobs, glassmorphism everywhere, or excessive blur.
- No cartoon robots, circuit-board stock imagery, random AI-generated headshots, or meaningless decorative code.
- No inflated claims such as “expert,” “industry-leading,” “state-of-the-art,” or “production-ready” unless the underlying work supports them.
- No fake company logos, fabricated testimonials, made-up client work, fake metrics, or fictional demo links.
- No giant stack of skill badges or endless technology logo wall.
- No animation that makes reading slower or harms accessibility.
- Do not describe every project as an “AI-powered platform.” Be specific about what it actually does.

## 3. Visual direction

### Design concept

**Graphite research notebook meets an independent audio-technology studio.**

Use the visual restraint of a premium engineering publication and the atmosphere of a carefully designed creative tool. The website can have a subtle tactile quality, but must remain clean and legible.

### Colour system

Use a very small palette:

- **Page background:** near-black graphite, for example `#0B0C0D` or `#0D0F10`.
- **Elevated surfaces:** `#121516` / `#171A1B`.
- **Main text:** warm off-white, for example `#E8E8E2`.
- **Secondary text:** muted grey, for example `#9A9E9C`.
- **Low-contrast borders:** `#292D2D`.
- **Single restrained accent:** desaturated steel / mineral blue-grey, for example `#8298A3`, or another subdued cool accent if the visual system needs it.

Keep the page predominantly monochrome. The accent is for small active states, fine rules, links, chart highlights, selected labels, and tiny interface details only. **Do not use electric blue, bright cyan, purple gradients, hot pink, or lime as dominant colours.** Avoid introducing multiple accent colours.

Check text contrast against WCAG AA wherever practical.

### Texture and atmosphere

- A very subtle grain/noise texture may sit over the background at low opacity.
- Use thin borders, hairline separators, subtle grid hints, and carefully aligned layout edges.
- If using a grid, it should be almost invisible and should not resemble a sci-fi HUD.
- Use one or two carefully selected technical visual motifs at most: a waveform line, a coordinate-like annotation, a tiny experiment label, or a data-grid detail.
- The texture must never reduce readability, create visual noise, or impact load performance.

### Typography

Use a deliberate, limited type system:

- **Headlines:** a confident modern grotesk/sans family with good display weight. Examples: Geist, Inter, or a comparable available family.
- **Body copy:** a clean readable sans, such as Inter or the same family with appropriate weights.
- **Metadata/code labels:** a restrained monospace, such as IBM Plex Mono or Geist Mono.

Use large editorial headings, short line lengths, comfortable body line-height, and small uppercase metadata with careful tracking. Do not overuse all-caps. Do not use a sci-fi or pixel font.

Use locally available or performance-conscious fonts where possible, with sensible system fallbacks. Do not introduce a font dependency solely for decoration.

### Layout

- Use a centred content container with a generous but responsive max-width.
- Use a strong 12-column editorial grid on wide screens if the chosen framework supports it naturally.
- Align section headings, cards, and separators precisely.
- Leave negative space. Do not cram every project, skill, and detail into the first viewport.
- Project cards should vary in layout based on their content, but still belong to one coherent system.
- Make the website feel crafted, not like a collection of unrelated cards.

### Motion

Use motion as quiet feedback, not entertainment:

- Smooth anchor scrolling where reduced-motion settings allow it.
- Small opacity/translate transitions on project cards and links.
- A restrained hover border or background change.
- A short, subtle hero entrance only if it feels intentional.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- No cursor-following spotlight, parallax overload, long intro animation, animated gradient, or scroll-jacking.
- Do not hide core content behind animation.

#### What the project covers

- Mel-spectrogram-based audio representation.
- A CNN trained from scratch as a baseline.

- Five-fold evaluation using the dataset’s official fold structure.
- Shared preprocessing for training and inference.
- A prediction script and a Gradio interface for local experimentation.

#### Results that may be shown

Present results with precise labels and enough context. Do not flatten the results into one ambiguous “accuracy” number.

**Five-fold ResNet18 evaluation:**

| Fold | Accuracy |
|---|---:|
| Fold 1 | 71.50% |
| Fold 2 | 78.75% |
| Fold 3 | 73.00% |
| Fold 4 | 80.75% |
| Fold 5 | 74.75% |
| **Mean across five folds** | **75.75%** |

Population standard deviation across folds: **3.48 percentage points**.

Display this as:

`75.75%` — mean accuracy across five official folds  
`5 folds` — evaluation coverage  
`50 classes` — ESC-50 categories

A concise note may explain that the five-fold mean is more informative than a single-fold score.

**Optional fold-5 architecture comparison, if the card or case-study page has room:**

- Baseline CNN: 46.50%.
- CNN + SpecAugment: 37.50%.
- ResNet18 final checkpoint: 68.25%.

Keep the phrase **“fold-5 comparison”** next to those values. Do not frame this single-fold table as the five-fold result.

**Important evaluation integrity note:** a separate ResNet18 checkpoint selected using the validation fold reached 76.75% on that same fold. This is diagnostic, not an unbiased comparison; do not use it as the headline result or present it alongside the five-fold mean as if it were directly comparable.

Tone: clear, grounded, curious, technically literate, and quietly confident.

Prefer:
- “I tested…”
- “The experiment compares…”
- “The workflow explores…”
- “The current prototype…”

- “Revolutionary”
- “Disrupting the future”
- “Next-generation AI”
- “Unleashing limitless potential”
- “World-class”

Each project card should quickly answer:
1. What is it?
2. What question or problem does it address?
3. What did Brettlee build or investigate?
4. What evidence can the visitor inspect?
5. What is the project's current status?

Be especially careful with ML metrics: label the dataset, evaluation method, fold context, and whether a number is single-fold or averaged. Do not make comparisons that the experiment does not support.
